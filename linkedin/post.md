What does faith have to do with health?

I explored this question using the 2022 General Social Survey, comparing U.S. adults who identify as Christian, with another faith, or with no religious affiliation.

The picture is more honest—and more human—than a slogan. About 72% of Christian adults reported good or excellent general health, compared with 76% among adults of other faiths and 71% among the religiously unaffiliated. Differences were more visible in mental health: 15% of Christians reported eight or more mentally unhealthy days in the previous month, versus 23% among other-faith respondents and 25% among the unaffiliated.

These are associations, not proof that faith causes health. The other-faith
group is smaller, so its estimate is less precise. Age, income, community,
access to care, and many other realities matter. Christianity does not promise
a life without illness, and suffering is not evidence of weak faith.

But the gospel offers something deeper than a technique for controlling our bodies. It gives us a God who sustains us in weakness, often through ordinary gifts: physicians, rest, friendship, prayer, and a community that refuses to let us suffer alone.

“They shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.” — Isaiah 40:31 (KJV)

Where have you seen faith and community support wellbeing without turning health into a measure of spiritual worth?
